Fifteen works. Five in this hall, of light, language, learning, life and sound, and ten more in the rooms beyond it. None of it is a picture or a recording. Every frame is worked out on your device as you watch, by code an AI wrote from a two-line brief. Go on, touch things.

Quicksilver

2026. Geometry solved per pixel on your graphics card, 0 × 0 pixels, 60 frames a second.

Move across it. A drop breaks away and follows you.

There is no film playing up there. That metal has no file. It is a short description of a shape, solved again for every pixel of every frame. When you move, it has to be solved differently.

Murmuration

2026. 0 points of light, each steering toward a letter, 60 frames a second.

Type anything and the flock rearranges itself to say it. Then push your way through it.

Next room3. Study for a Mind→

Each point knows two things: where it is going, and what is in its way. Nobody draws the letters. They are what is left when tens of thousands of small decisions agree.

Study for a Mind

2026. A neural network of 0 connections, written from scratch, learning on your device. Step 0, 0% right.

It starts out knowing nothing. Watch it work out which dots are amber and which are blue, and redraw its map each time it changes its mind. Click or tap the map to add a dot. It will have to reconsider.

Next room4. Turing’s Garden→

This is the idea that runs the largest models, at a size small enough to watch. Make a guess. Measure how wrong it was. Nudge every connection slightly toward less wrong. Repeat a few hundred times.

Turing's Garden

2026. Two chemicals, two rules, 0 cells, 0 generations a second.

In 1952 Alan Turing proposed that spots, stripes and coral could all grow from two substances chasing each other. He was right. Move across the dark to plant some.

Next room5. Aeolian Harp→

The same man who asked whether machines could think also explained how the leopard gets its spots. Nothing above is drawn. Each cell looks only at its neighbours, and the garden happens anyway.

Aeolian Harp

2026. 0 strings, synthesised live. No recordings. 0 notes played so far.

An aeolian harp is played by the wind. This one is played by a machine that was never taught a tune, and by you. Turn the sound on, then run across the strings.

On a keyboard, focus the strings, then use the arrow keys, or space for a run across them.
