Necklaces

2026. Four rings, 0 beats a minute, four beats to a turn. Every sound is synthesised at the moment it is struck.

Each ring spreads its beats as evenly as they will go. Add or take away a beat and the ring rearranges itself, and more often than not the result already has a name somewhere in the world. Take hold of a ring to turn it against the others. Turn the sound on.

In 2005 the computer scientist Godfried Toussaint pointed out that if you spread some beats as evenly as possible round a circle, using a method Euclid wrote down for another purpose entirely, you get rhythms people have played for centuries: the Cuban tresillo, the bossa nova, the bell pattern of West Africa. Nobody designed them that way. Evenness just sounds right.
