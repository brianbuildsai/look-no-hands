A Shadow of a Shadow

2026. The 120-cell: 600 corners, 1,200 edges, and 120 dodecahedra for sides. A solid with four dimensions, flattened twice to get here.

A cube’s shadow on a wall is a flat drawing of something with one more dimension than the wall has. This is the same trick, played twice. The solid is turning in a direction you do not have, which is why it seems to pass through itself and turn inside out; it is doing neither. Drag to turn it yourself. If it is too much, work up from the 5-cell: there are exactly six of these, and no more.

Next room25. A Thousand Guesses→

Ludwig Schläfli found all six of these in about 1850, in Bern, without ever seeing one; the work was not printed whole until after his death. In the 1880s Alicia Boole Stott, who had no mathematical schooling at all, found them again by picturing their slices, and gave English the word polytope. Neither had a screen to turn one on.
