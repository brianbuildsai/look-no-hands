Undercroft

2026. A game engine of sixteen-pixel tiles and a sixtieth-of-a-second step, sprites compiled from text when the page opened, floors stitched from a seeded grammar. Floor 0, seed 36, 0 milliseconds a frame.

When two play, nothing about the game crosses the wire but the buttons each of you presses: both machines compute the whole undercroft from the same seed and compare notes twice a second, so a guardian written next year will work for two without knowing there are two. Only finding each other needs help (a public broker passes the first handshake along and then is out of it), unless your two networks will not let each other in, in which case a public message broker, of the kind run for people trying out thermostats, passes the buttons along too and you play a sixth of a second in the past. The sound effects are the one thing here not made on your machine: about sixty short recordings generated by ElevenLabs from written prompts (the words are in tools/sounds.js), fetched only when you ask for sound; the music is still synthesised as it plays. Rogue was written by Michael Toy and Glenn Wichman in 1980 for a Unix machine that drew its dungeon in letters, a new one each time, and threw away the game when you died, which is the lineage of everything here. The platformer half is older than the arcade: Donkey Kong in 1981 was the first to be called one. The undercroft borrows its seeded rooms from the first, its coyote time and jump buffer from the second, and its lantern from room 28.
